# Abstract

E-commerce microservice architectures generate dozens of simultaneous anomaly alerts during degradation events, yet existing alert prioritization methods rank alerts solely by instantaneous anomaly severity and static service criticality — treating a memory leak that has been building for 15 minutes identically to a transient CPU spike that self-resolves in seconds. This temporal blindness causes engineers to investigate low-risk transient alerts first while revenue-critical persistent faults escalate undetected. We introduce PersistRank, a duration-aware alert prioritization framework that incorporates a novel persistence score — quantifying how many consecutive time windows a service has remained anomalous — into the risk computation alongside anomaly severity and business criticality. Evaluated on the RS-Anomic benchmark comprising 12 microservices, 10 distinct fault injection types, and over 123,000 monitoring windows, PersistRank achieves a Precision@3 of 0.514, outperforming the severity-criticality baseline (0.442) by 7.2 percentage points, with the largest gains observed on slow-building network degradation faults such as response-time delays (+18.9 pp), out-of-order packets (+13.3 pp), and low-bandwidth conditions (+11.2 pp). An ablation study confirms that persistence is an independent and complementary signal: removing it reduces Precision@3 by 14.0%, while the full three-factor model consistently ranks the true affected service higher than any single- or two-factor alternative, demonstrating that how long a service has been degraded is as important as how severely it is degraded for effective e-commerce alert triage.

**Keywords:** alert prioritization, microservice monitoring, anomaly persistence, e-commerce, unsupervised anomaly detection, Isolation Forest
